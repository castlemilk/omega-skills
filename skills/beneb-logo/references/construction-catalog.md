## Use the catalog

Treat these names as shorthand for drawable construction seeds. They are not approved assets, a mandatory menu, recorded user preferences or proof that a rendered image satisfies its premise. Invent and compare new branches from the same discipline.

Use one operation and one tension per mark. State parts, voids, joins and termini in the individual prompt. Keep company credibility, flat monochrome geometry, purposeful darkness and accurate subordinate typography across families.

## Plan diversity

For a request to explore different logos, compare the entire planned set before rendering. In a ten-image batch, spread directions through several families and cover contrasting values of these variables; do not require every possible combination:

| Variable | Useful contrasts |
| --- | --- |
| Silhouette | Wide and low; tall and narrow; compact; irregular with one projecting shoulder. |
| Material organisation | One connected mass; two reciprocal pieces; several aligned elements; a sparse framework. |
| Space | Open passage; fully enclosed void; several chambers; nested positive and negative forms. |
| Rhythm | Broad flowing bend; angular displacement; compressed joint; one controlled interruption. |
| Balance | Reflection; 180-degree rotation; another explicit symmetry rule; deliberate asymmetry. |
| Scale relationship | Single resolution; nested reversals; an emblem and a field derived from the same rule. |

Flag a cluster whose only differences are title, colour, material, stripe count or a small notch. Change the construction rather than adding decoration. Do not make ten parallel waves or ten clipped enclosures simply because one reference uses that silhouette.

Apply this breadth check to radical or expressly varied exploration. Preserve the user's selected geometry in refinement; adaptive comparisons may intentionally share a family. General approval does not convert every prior direction into a selected parent.

## Path operations

| Seed | Construction and tension |
| --- | --- |
| **Field** | Route three or four substantial ordered paths through one smooth displacement. Preserve their sequence and flat terminals. Let continuity across a change of alignment supply the identity. |
| **Shear** | Stagger the start of short angular transitions along parallel paths. Re-establish a shared direction after the displacement. Compare ordered spacing with a shifted outer contour. |
| **Aperture** | Route paths around an off-centre opening, then regain alignment. Name which side remains open and which boundaries are enclosed. Let the protected absence organise the whole. |
| **Convergence** | Merge independent routes at unequal positions into one shared terminal. Keep the negative channels legible until their intended endings. Compare independent origins with shared structure. |
| **Phase** | Interrupt a coherent route system at one registered boundary; resume it with a deliberate offset. Keep the shared cut visible in the miniature. Compare apparent continuity with an authored break. |
| **Inflection** | Use one uninterrupted substantial path with one decisive change of direction and unequal terminal heights. Make the open interior and outer silhouette distinctive; avoid defaulting to a generic wave or loop. |
| **Interchange** | Organise two unequal offset openings with a sparse set of routes, one controlled connection and clearly located ends. Compare multiple chambers with a coherent whole. Avoid ambiguous crossings and decorative circuitry. |

## Reciprocal and adjacent forms

| Seed | Construction and tension |
| --- | --- |
| **Counterpath** | Arrange substantial hooked routes in a reciprocal relationship, such as 180-degree rotation. Let their ends pass at different heights across a deliberate interior gap. Compare coordinated movement with separation. |
| **Adjacency** | Place several unequal bent elements so their terminals imply a route across equal or deliberately related gaps. Keep every part disconnected if that is the rule. Compare visual connection with material separation. |
| **Register** | Assemble angular parts on displaced axes while aligning their inner terminals to one shared diagonal. Let the outer silhouette remain unfamiliar. Compare local order with a change of reference frame. |
| **Splice** | Bring two broad, low tongues from opposite directions toward an oblique seam. Keep one subtle difference of height or reach. Compare apparent transfer across a joint with an actual gap; preserve the unusually wide proportion. |

## Invent the next branch

Choose a useful family, then change a structural variable: route ownership, boundary permeability, merger order, component count, nested polarity, terminal placement or the relationship between axes. State the resulting silhouette and negative-space event before naming the concept.

A speculative premise such as connectivity independent of distance needs a visible consequence, for example aligned ends that imply a route across an unusually large interval. A memory operation can leave one persistent cut. Do not add decorative equations to explain a weak form.

For an emblem-plus-field study, apply the same rule at another density or scale. For a logo-only comparison, keep the board quiet. In either case, inspect the actual result and describe the construction that was rendered, including departures from the plan.
