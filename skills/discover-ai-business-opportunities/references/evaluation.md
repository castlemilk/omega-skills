# Evidence and evaluation

## Keep evidence types distinct

| Label | What it supports | What it does not support |
|---|---|---|
| Verified transaction or contractual purchase | A particular buyer purchased a particular scope | Market size, margins or willingness to buy your offer |
| Reported commercial activity | Attributed revenue, volume, delivered sessions or usage | Audited outcomes unless actually audited; AI causality |
| First-person purchase/cancellation report | A claimed purchase, use, churn or workaround | Receipt verification or representative incidence |
| Advertised offer/price | Competitor scope, list price, terms and availability | Sales, retention or achievable pricing for a new entrant |
| Specific pain or job request | A concrete problem and possibly a buyer | A budget or completed purchase |
| Survey | Findings within the stated sampling frame | Population prevalence without methodological support |
| Hypothetical interest or promotion | A lead to investigate | Validated demand |
| Inference/scenario | Your reasoning conditional on assumptions | An observed fact or dated forecast |

A source can support several narrow claims at different confidence levels. Record which. Do not infer USD from a dollar sign, equate bookings/GMV with revenue, equate ARR with audited profit, or turn an isolated forum allegation into a finding about a whole provider. Retain contradictory evidence and possible commercial incentives.

## Demand and competitive map

For the finalist, distinguish:
- What people say they want, actually use, already buy, stop buying, and attempt themselves.
- User, payer and budget owner; one may be different from the others.
- The current total cost of the job, the available spend and your feasible price.
- A growing activity from a growing monetizable market.
- A one-off cleanup from repeated work; a subscription should earn its renewal.

Check the closest direct competitor, a bundled incumbent option, and the buyer's internal/free/do-nothing alternative where they exist. Missing competitors in a search is not an empty market. A vendor adding agents is a substitution risk, not proof that all services disappear.

## Business design

Capture a concise offer: buyer segment, triggering event, inputs/access, completed result, acceptance, exclusions, customer approvals and exception owner. Ask why the buyer delegates even if their own AI could perform the analysis.

Use unit economics only to expose important assumptions:

Contribution per job = net price minus delivery labour at replacement cost, tools/API, travel, consumables, variable support, expected rework/cover, transaction/platform fees and attributable capital wear.

Acquisition, onboarding, fixed overhead, insurance and financing still need coverage. Model a range when evidence is weak. For recurring work, show utilization and churn/renewal sensitivity; for physical work, show route density, capacity and working capital. Do not present the formula as a complete financial forecast or invent conversion, retention, margin, TAM or willingness-to-pay figures.

Start with existing tools, manual service delivery or a suitable partner when that tests demand faster. Bespoke software or hardware needs a demonstrated gap. A proposed service-level guarantee must match what delivery can control. Flag regulatory, licensing, safety and funding-route questions without assuming permission or offering a legal conclusion.

## Scenario tests

1. **Concentrated/expensive AI:** dependency, supplier pricing, access and concentration risk.
2. **Cheap widespread intelligence:** software copying, internal substitution, integration/migration costs collapse, customers negotiate harder.
3. **Cheap intelligence plus robotics:** field labour or installation advantages may weaken; inspect rights, geography, utilization and economics, not just hardware ownership.
4. **Purchasing-power stress:** fewer employees, lower professional incomes, stronger price pressure, or a different institutional payer. Essential need does not guarantee funding.

Ask what accumulates with each delivery: repeat purchases, demonstrated acquisition channel, lower total delivery cost, accepted evidence, contractual rights, useful installed capacity or referenceable results. Distinguish proposed assets from assets actually owned. Access permission is not exclusivity; data must be lawfully useful and hard to replace; network value requires real participation or transactions.

## Final decision and experiment

Choose among `lead`, `investigate`, `test`, `watch`, `rejected`, and `active`. Explain the decisive reason and confidence. Do not let an aggregate score hide missing budget or unsafe delivery.

A useful experiment names the target buyer, the assumption at risk, the smallest observation or offer, effort/cost, evidence that would advance it, and a stopping condition. Asking about the last purchase or inspecting an authorized redacted work order is stronger than asking “Would you use this?” A proposed paid pilot is not authorization to sell or accept money.

Three unrelated paying customers across three relevant cycles may be a useful learning target, not statistical validation. Episodic work should use repeat orders or referrals instead. Rejections are learning: save the cause so the next cycle does not merely rename the same idea.
