Use creative writing to discover a scene's reason to exist. Keep the loop bounded and make images when images are requested. This is a method for original fiction and art direction, not a claim that the technologies are real or scientifically demonstrated.

## Operating modes

| Request | Apply |
| --- | --- |
| Invent new worlds or an exploratory image set | Full premise-to-image loop; vary underlying worlds and design languages |
| Develop one established civilization | Preserve canon; explore new institutions, locations, activities and viewpoints |
| One open image | Two or three brief seeds, one selected card, one image |
| Art-led diagram, cutaway or world-system image | Full visual invention within the inherited sci-fi direction; render the world, then compose exact annotations separately if requested |
| Explicit technical/vector/data diagram | Preserve exact semantics with a suitable precise renderer; use the optional schematic examples only when they fit |
| Exact subject, format or composition | Add only reasoning that serves the brief; do not introduce extra lore or subjects |
| Edit an existing image | Preserve specified invariants and use the actual image; bypass wholesale reinvention |
| Concepts or writing only | Deliver concepts or prose; do not generate images |
| Prompts only | Develop the concept as needed, then provide prompts; do not generate images |

Before selecting open concepts, run the [deliberate-departure pass](antialgorithm-method.md#run-one-bounded-creative-pass). Preserve fixed choices, give each departure a subject-specific reason, and compare the main design move with the actual available notebook. Do not use randomness as evidence of originality.

For a series, draft approximately twice as many one-sentence candidates as final images. Work in batches of at most twelve selected scenes when the request is larger, maintaining a set-wide diversity ledger. Develop only promising candidates. Default to one draft, one critique and one targeted revision before rendering. Do not require concept approval unless the user asks for it. Do not multiply image calls merely to exhaust a quota; make the requested number and revise real failures.

## Preserve the selected direction

Before inventing candidates, establish the visual direction from the request and [monumental-direction.md](monumental-direction.md). For the default exploration, retain monumental science-fiction presence, convincing scale, coherent material construction and atmospheric depth. Inspect references supplied in the current task when available; use the bundled text atlas for proposed direction otherwise. Treat this direction as a selection criterion throughout the loop, not a style adjective added to the final prompt.

Explore unrestricted energy, time, technology and unusual forms of existence within that direction. Translate care, play, diplomacy or unfamiliar perception into substantial worlds and scenes. Do not substitute a small decorative plaza, bead track or abstract diagram for the requested epic imagery merely because its accompanying story is original. Keep explicit product, intimate-scene and style-change requests as overrides. The word “diagram” alone does not change this art direction into a technical plate or a dashboard. Before rendering, remove labels and ask whether the construction could be an ordinary bridge, turbine or textbook globe; if so, rebuild its alien morphology, setting and scale relationships.

## 1. Establish a consequential premise

Start with a changed condition of existence, not a list of shapes. Ask: **What can happen here that changes how a civilization lives?** Choose one primary premise and optionally one interacting premise. Examples include navigable time, inhabited fields, self-authoring ecosystems, civilizations that migrate as oceans, or the deliberate creation of physical laws.

Select the fictional regime from the brief:

- **Grounded extrapolation:** retain requested physical or practical constraints; research real technical claims when necessary.
- **Far-future speculation:** extend known ideas freely, distinguishing the extrapolation from established science.
- **Unbounded invention:** accept the requested abundance of energy, time, space and technological ability. Permit invented physics and cosmic-scale agency. Maintain consistency with the selected premise without adding obligatory fuel, money or lifespan shortages.

Do not ask what a limitless civilization must lack before allowing it to be interesting. Ask what it chooses, values, encounters, creates, preserves or refuses. Finite forms can be voluntary expressions of taste, ritual, relationship or purpose. A fragile material may be cherished rather than technologically unavoidable.

State each candidate as a causal sentence when agency matters:

> Because [extraordinary capability], [inhabitants] choose [purpose or practice], creating [unexpected visible consequence].

For an uninhabited, emergent or unexplained world, use an alternative:

> Under [unusual condition], [process or observed relationship] produces [ecological or physical consequence], visible when [scene event].

Keep unknown makers, intentions and mechanisms unknown. An unfamiliar landscape or relic does not need an invented civic explanation to earn an image.

Remove empty substitutes for invention. "An ancient quantum megastructure" provides age, jargon and size but no premise. Replace it with what the structure changes and why someone wants that change.

## 2. Give existence a point of view

Use inhabitants and institutions when they matter to the premise. For natural worlds, choose an ecological or observational point of view without assigning personhood or a designer. For mysterious artifacts, distinguish a visible pattern from a guessed purpose.

Choose a meaningful pair or three of the following rather than filling every field mechanically:

- **Identity:** individual organisms, symbiotic ecologies, distributed minds, temporary collectives, weather systems, intelligences with no stable bodies.
- **Perception:** light, chemistry, gravity, vibration, memory, alternative futures, patterns that humans cannot directly perceive.
- **Desire:** exploration, stewardship, hospitality, beauty, play, remembrance, transformation, recognition, solitude, protection, conquest or voluntary disappearance.
- **Institution:** a migration custom, archive, garden, embassy, game, family form, scientific practice, ritual, court or shared work.
- **Attitude:** delight, patience, reverence, ambivalence, unease, indifference, grief, determination, curiosity or wonder.

Let inhabitants have agency. Avoid treating every unfamiliar species as a monster, enemy or human society with different masks. A civilization may be sympathetic, incomprehensible, ethically complex or simply occupied with something unrelated to people.

Use user-supplied franchise references as creative lenses, not borrowed content. For example, select ecological interdependence and deep history, industrial intimacy and biological unease, unfamiliar perception and pursuit, mythic movement and cultural contrast, or optimistic discovery and ethical encounter. These are optional art-direction abstractions, not an authoritative analysis of those franchises. Do not copy named factions, character archetypes with identifying details, signature anatomy, ships, insignia or plots when the request is for new worlds.

## 3. Develop consequences and history

Connect the idea across a short chain:

`capability -> motive/value -> social or ecological practice -> physical form -> event`

Or, for emergence without known intent:

`condition -> process -> ecological or physical consequence -> scene`

Choose two or three consequences. Give at least one a visual expression beyond decorative style:

| World fact | Possible visible expression |
| --- | --- |
| Citizens live as pressure patterns in an ocean | Habitation follows moving water boundaries; walls and doorways may be absent |
| A culture preserves different rates of time | Adjacent regions show contrasting growth, erosion and motion, separated by chosen boundaries |
| Cities are temporary collective bodies | Buildings detach into purposeful migrating forms while shared spaces remain open |
| Transport changes spatial adjacency | Distant geographies meet at a sharp transition; the ship's wake is rearranged space rather than flame |
| Materials continuously repair themselves | Smooth renewed surfaces contrast with deliberately preserved historical scars |
| A defense instrument removes possibilities | Power appears through interrupted shadows, missing volumes or changed relationships rather than a bigger cannon |

For an organic-techno brief, add one functional bridge to the causal chain: identify the biological process, technological role, convergence stage and visible shared structure. Make growth, circulation, sensing, repair or adaptation materially change the design. Use the [convergence method](concept-space.md#convergent-organic-technology); avoid relying on an animal silhouette, decorative vines or a technological label to establish fusion.

Add one trace of history: an earlier construction method, a voluntarily retained scar, a former ecosystem, a ritual repeated through geological ages, or a structure whose current use differs from its origin. Do not equate age with rust. Indefinite maintenance, renewal, burial, overgrowth and cultural reinterpretation provide different histories.

Tension is optional. If useful, derive it from incompatible aspirations, consent, belonging, interpretation, unfamiliar life, competing aesthetics, the meaning of memory or the right to remain unchanged. A celebration or successful act of care can carry the scene. Do not force conflict into a request for serenity.

Keep invented technology at the level of fictional capability, visible behavior and narrative consequences. Concept art for propulsion or weapons does not require operational real-world construction details.

## 4. Find the image moment

Write a short scene paragraph in the present tense. Include an action or a meaningful state, a viewpoint, one environmental behavior and something implied beyond the frame. Use specific sensations and relationships; avoid a synopsis of an entire civilization.

Then select **one event or relationship that an image can carry**:

- Arrival, departure, unfolding, repair, a first encounter, a shared ceremony, an awakening, a discovery, an unfinished transformation or a quiet aftermath.
- A tiny known object beside a vast unknown one, a world casting a shadow on its own construction, or a material changing behavior across a boundary.
- A familiar environment made strange by one coherent intervention.

Name two or three visible consequences of the premise. If the premise exists only in a caption, revise the moment or choose a different one. Some facts such as political consent or historical identity cannot be proven visually; express them through accompanying fiction without claiming the image verifies them.

Choose scale language deliberately. Use bodily cues for requested intimate scenes, ecological relationships for nonhuman habitats, weather and geology for planetary scenes, and occlusion, curvature and nested distances for astronomical scenes. For default monumental work, make at least two compatible scale relationships visible when useful: a small vessel under a vast overhang and clouds beneath its farther spans, for example. Deliberately uncertain total scale may serve a mystery, but local mass and depth must still feel consequential. Do not add astronauts, windows and railings by habit.

## 5. Audit originality and breadth

First remove titles and lore and check that each pictured moment still fits the intended theme. An attractive but unrelated toy, plaza, tabletop or diagrammatic scene fails this check; more imaginative lore cannot compensate. Then strip off titles and color words to compare the two most similar surviving candidates. A nominal score is less useful than naming the repetition.

| Dimension | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Premise | Do multiple concepts depend on the same capability with different nouns? |
| Purpose | Within the requested ships, structures, monoliths or other domains, do their purposes and events actually differ? Broaden the reasons to exist without abandoning requested subjects. |
| Inhabitants | Are all worlds designed for the same imagined human scale and senses? |
| Form | Do repeated rings, wedges, spires, pods or centered hero objects dominate the set? |
| Environment | Are the scenes merely the same object against different clouds? |
| Material | Has every advanced civilization become painted metal with service pipes? |
| Moment | Are all scenes passive fly-bys? Does every active scene become a battle? |
| Composition | Do viewpoint, crop, horizon, depth and negative-space shape actually change? |
| Mood and palette | Is every world grim, orange/teal, dusty or illuminated by cyan engines? |

As a soft heuristic, selected neighbors should differ meaningfully on several conceptual and visual dimensions. Do not force a numerical diversity score or a category quota that damages the brief. Preserve intentional similarities for a connected series.

Track every explicitly requested subject domain across the selected set. A request spanning ships, planetary structures, propulsion and weapons should not become an exclusively ecological or philosophical series. For small counts, combine compatible domains in one scene. Do not force any unrequested category into a precise brief.

Repair repeated concepts at their cause. Change what the civilization does, how matter organizes, how the technology manifests, or which event is shown before changing the paint color. Compare exposed interiors as well as silhouettes: different subject names can conceal the same ribbed construction. Keep approved craft qualities, such as tactile mark quality and readable detail hierarchy, while varying the [construction language](concept-space.md#change-the-construction-language) when exploring unrelated worlds.

## 6. Compile the image brief

For open worldbuilding artwork, show a compact selected-concept slate before generation: title plus one or two sentences describing the world and pictured moment. Continue directly to generation without making the slate an approval checkpoint. Preserve the bypass for precise scenes and edits, and obey requests for image-only delivery.

Translate the selected card into visual instructions in this order:

1. Format, selected theme, concrete subject and decisive scene relationship.
2. One sentence of premise that changes visible behavior.
3. Organizing silhouette, distributed system or boundary; supporting rhythm, viewpoint, crop and depth.
4. Two or three concrete visual consequences.
5. Scale evidence appropriate to the inhabitants and environment, plus the depth or cropped continuation that implies a larger world.
6. Material response, localized details and age or maintenance logic.
7. Palette roles, motivated light, atmosphere and finish.
8. User constraints and only a few relevant exclusions.

Remove lore the image cannot depict. Avoid including the whole concept bank, proper-noun glossary or a demand to visualize multiple epochs simultaneously unless that is the chosen premise. If a world has several compelling scenes, save the alternatives for later rather than cramming them into one frame.

## 7. Generate and inspect

Use the current image-generation tool through its supported interface. Make separate images when separate images are requested. Preserve edit targets and user constraints. Await all calls and display actual outputs; do not count prompts as images or silently replace requested renders with diagrams.

Inspect the actual render:

- **Theme fit first:** the image reads as the requested science-fiction world at the intended scale without relying on its caption. Reject toy, decorative courtyard or abstract-diagram drift before polishing detail.
- **Immediate read:** silhouette, focal point, scale, emotional register and distinctive composition.
- **Construction:** overlap, attachment, material behavior, detail hierarchy and plausible visual relationships within the fiction.
- **World expression:** the selected observable consequences and event. Identify when a generator flattened an unfamiliar concept into a standard ship, city, portal or industrial corridor.

If a material failure occurs, perform one focused revision that preserves successful features. Prefer edits of the actual output when preserving it matters. If the whole visual concept fails the requested theme and no user invariants require it, replace the composition. If a concept remains beyond what the image communicates, say what the image shows and keep the richer premise as fiction; do not claim full success through a caption alone. Respect user-requested additional iteration.

## 8. Carry learning forward

Keep a compact notebook of selected world facts, design fingerprints, exact final prompts when useful, render status and observed mismatches. In a connected series, label established facts and new proposals. In exploration, record repeated motifs to avoid in the next batch.

Translate feedback into specific dimensions: "broader ship topology and more nonhuman worlds" is more useful than "make everything epic." After positive feedback, inspect the approved image and name the spatial, material and atmospheric relationships that worked. Carry those qualities into the next prompt while changing its premise and construction when exploration is requested. Compare actual renders again; a planned difference may disappear during generation. Do not let one successful image impose its palette, ruins, rings or material system on every later world. Do not claim persistent learning or saved memory unless an authorized persistent artifact or skill update was actually made.

## Worked causal example

**Premise:** A civilization can temporarily join distant environments without moving either one.

**Value and practice:** Its citizens regard hospitality as sharing weather and ecology. They organize voluntary seasonal exchanges between otherwise isolated worlds.

**Visible consequence:** A bridge is unnecessary. A mountain valley ends at a precise suspended boundary beyond which a foreign ocean rises vertically; local trees lean toward salt mist, while marine organisms gather on the other side.

**Moment:** The first exchange opens at dawn. A small flock crosses from mountain air into a sheltered corridor through the alien sea. The boundary frame is largely empty space, with a few smooth grown anchors whose branching geometry follows the two environments.

**Image brief:** Use a low valley viewpoint, a vast upright ocean plane entering from the right, distant mountain light at left and tiny migrating organisms near the meeting line. Express scale through cloud shadows and shoreline curvature, with clear water, deep green vegetation and sparse pearl anchors. The concept becomes visible through the environmental meeting; its diplomacy remains accompanying fiction.

Do not reuse this valley, upright ocean or anchor geometry as the universal answer. Reuse the causal method.
