## Principle

Build complexity from repeatable rules. Choose one structural rhythm, two or three materials, and a small set of recurring details. Make close inspection reward the viewer without destroying the distant silhouette. The vocabulary below is original authoring guidance. Gaskets, clamps, machining methods and fictional functions are proposed details, not observations from an unspecified reference or validated engineering.

## Form and structural rhythm

| Form | Structural development | Useful prompt fragment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annular opening | Concentric collars, radial ribs, recessed throat | “Three offset collars frame a dark opening; the innermost ribs diminish into shadow.” |
| Monolithic slab | Recessed bays, deep edge thickness, stepped undercuts | “A nearly unbroken upper plane ends in a densely serviced underside.” |
| Spire | Stacked decks, diminishing modules, mast, radial platforms | “Decks become narrower toward the tip, with a few broad service rings near the base.” |
| Swept sail | Thin edge, reinforced root, tensioned curve | “A long curved plane meets a narrow mechanical root; seams follow the sweep.” |
| Suspended pod | Rounded shell, cutaway underside, repeating attachment | “Pale capsule ends repeat at uneven intervals along a common service spine.” |
| Industrial canyon | Stacked frames, interlocking terraces, deep circulation slot | “Dark transverse frames interrupt a long luminous void.” |
| Organic shell | Ribbed folds, asymmetrical aperture, interwoven tendons | “The aperture folds inward in irregular segments; thicker ribs gather around its base.” |
| Deep shaft | Foreshortened grid, nested ledges, light at depth | “Repeated rectangular levels become finer and lower in contrast as the shaft descends.” |

Let details inherit form: use radial seams on circles, flowing seams on a sail, orthogonal service channels on slabs, and irregular-but-related ribs on an organic body. Avoid unrelated geometry at every scale.

## Intricate mechanical details

Select three to six details for a normal scene. Add more only for a close technical view where they remain readable.

| Detail | Placement and connection | Visual contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recessed access hatch | Within a larger panel; latch on one edge, hinge on the other | Makes a broad surface feel serviceable |
| Paired fasteners | At corners or flange intervals | Establishes rhythm without sprinkling dots everywhere |
| Panel seams | Follow surface curvature and structural boundaries | Gives scale and assembly logic |
| Gasket line | Around a hatch or enclosed joint | Separates two materials with a narrow dark contour |
| Vent louvres | Beside a warm outlet or at a service bay | Adds a controlled repeated shadow pattern |
| Radiator fins | On an exposed flank with clear spacing | Gives heat-management character without claiming performance |
| Bundled cable runs | Between named modules; held by a few brackets | Links components into a system |
| Corrugated conduit | Across a joint or changing direction | Contrasts flexible routing with rigid plates |
| Braided hose | Around a constrained bend; connection ends visible | Adds tactile close detail |
| Circular coupling | At the end of a duct or hose | Provides a clear termination rather than a dangling tube |
| Service rails | Along a ledge or access route | Gives depth and human-scale inference |
| Ladders and steps | Between connected decks | Makes enormous structures traversable |
| Truss members | Across an opening with visible endpoints | Makes negative space structurally legible |
| Recessed windows | In a thin band or isolated bay | Gives scale with sparse warm or pale light |
| Docking recess | Deep cutout with guide surfaces and an entry path | Suggests use and a focal narrative |
| Stacked plates | At a root, corner, or high-stress-looking joint | Gives believable thickness |
| Small indicator lights | At an access control or docking edge | Adds emphasis without turning the whole object into neon |
| Serial marking | On a dedicated patch, only if required | Adds institutional character; exact text needs verification |

Describe where a cable starts and ends. Leave space around a hatch to imply access. Stop fins before a hinge. Let braces land on larger members. Use these as visual coherence checks, not an assertion that speculative machinery is buildable.

## Material response and age

| Material appearance | Light and surface behavior | Local age or wear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chalky painted alloy | Broad matte color, restrained edge glints | Chips at leading corners and hatch edges |
| Brushed metal | Directional highlights aligned with the form | Fine scratches near handled parts |
| Oxidized copper appearance | Mottled warm brown and muted green | Deposits concentrated at seams and drainage points |
| Ceramic-like shell | Smooth diffuse highlight, crisp dark joint | Sparse abrasions; keep most of the face clean |
| Dark composite appearance | Low reflectance, restrained grain | Polished patches around repeated contact areas |
| Ribbed flexible material | Repeating small highlights and occluded folds | Dust gathers in protected grooves |
| Mineral or stone-like mass | Broken planes, broad dry texture | Erosion follows exposed edges and vertical paths |
| Smoked glass | Dark transparency with selective reflections | Light dust, limited edge staining |
| Warm exposed framework | Thin luminous edge, deep internal shade | Uneven paint at joins and replacement panels |

Choose a history: newly commissioned, regularly maintained, long abandoned, or partially rebuilt. Then place wear to support it. Do not combine immaculate glass, pervasive rust, fresh paint everywhere, and total structural ruin without a reason. Keep wear intensity lower than structural contrast unless decay is the subject.

## Drawn and painted surface character

Use descriptions of visible finish instead of asserting a physical production method:

- **Fine technical linework:** narrow broken lines that suggest joints and levels; vary density with depth.
- **Crosshatched shadows:** short overlapping strokes inside recesses; keep light planes calmer.
- **Dry-brush-like edges:** incomplete strokes across an exposed bevel or weathered rim.
- **Scumbled color:** muted second-color flecks interrupt a large painted plane without becoming speckle noise.
- **Airbrushed atmosphere:** soft color transitions around haze and distant light; preserve sharper focal hardware.
- **Selective contour:** hard edges around the focal object, interrupted edges where the background shares its value.
- **Uneven pigment appearance:** slight color variation inside a field; avoid a uniform paper texture over the whole image.
- **Painterly compression:** resolve a distant service district into a few directional marks rather than miniature high-contrast components.

“A painted retrofuturist illustration with fine technical marks” is a medium direction. “An authentic hand-painted original” is a provenance claim; do not make it for generated work.

## Light, atmosphere, and scale

Use one main light mechanism and one optional accent:

- Broad warm sky against cool structure.
- High pale sidelight revealing relief without outlining every edge.
- A luminous inner throat illuminating only nearby rings.
- A cyan engine trail against a dark non-emissive hull.
- Small warm windows within a large cool mass.
- Haze that progressively reduces distant contrast.
- Deep shadow under a suspended form, with a narrow reflected light line.

Make scale through ratios and repetition. A tiny repair door beside a 20-deck wall, a railing against a vast portal, or progressively smaller craft can do more than the word “colossal.” Numeric ratios are optional design instructions, not source measurements. For a handheld object, use an identifiable button, fastener, edge thickness, and tabletop contact shadow instead of landscape-scale tricks.

## Detail packets

Insert one packet into a prompt, adjusting its location to the subject. Do not combine all of them.

### Service spine

> Keep the main shell smooth. Along its lower spine, add three recessed access bays joined by narrow cable runs, a few paired clamps, stepped metal lips, and a thin band of small warm windows. Let the outer edges catch light while the services remain mostly in shadow.

### Annular mechanism

> Divide the inner collar into repeated radial segments with deep rib shadows. Place small couplings at every fourth junction, reserve two gaps as service recesses, and add fine concentric scoring only near the rim. Keep the dark central opening visually clear.

### Maintained industrial surface

> Show layered painted panels with subtle tonal variation, intact gaskets around service hatches, limited abrasion on exposed corners, and darker stains directly below a few joints. Leave broad panel centers quiet and readable.

### Organic machine

> Make thick ribs converge toward the aperture and finer folds follow their direction. Route two flexible conduits into protected recesses, expose a darker inner membrane, and use uneven mineral discoloration along the thickest ridges. Keep the outer silhouette continuous.

### Atmospheric megastructure

> Resolve nearby terraces into railings, narrow doors, and separated support frames. Simplify the middle distance into repeated bands. Let the far architecture merge into colored haze, with only a few windows retained as scale cues.

### Clean product detail

> Use a restrained shell with one continuous gasket seam, a flush service cover, a precise vent rhythm, and a single recessed indicator. Concentrate small machining marks on the metal insert; keep the ceramic-like body clean.

## Replace vague adjectives

| Instead of | Write |
| --- | --- |
| “Extremely futuristic” | “A suspended annular habitat with a smooth outer shell and a deep ribbed interior.” |
| “Very intricate” | “Three nested service decks, connected conduits, small recessed hatches, and paired fasteners around the lower joint.” |
| “Epic scale” | “The doorway occupies a fraction of one terrace; upper levels vanish into haze.” |
| “Cinematic lighting” | “A low warm sun catches the outer edge while cool skylight fills the recessed face.” |
| “Amazing texture” | “Chalky paint breaks over the rim, with fine graphite-like seams across broad calm panels.” |
| “Cyberpunk glow” | “A single cyan channel lights the underside of the dark frame.” |
| “Realistic machinery” | “Each conduit ends at a coupling, braces meet visible supports, and panel seams follow the shell curvature.” |

## Avoid incompatible combinations

- Do not demand both a flat graphic poster and photographic surface realism without defining which layer uses which treatment.
- Do not request global pin-sharp microdetail together with deep atmospheric distance and shallow focus.
- Do not specify a calm minimalist image and then populate every background region with machinery.
- Do not cover a thin sail in bulky mechanisms that erase its defining contour.
- Do not add bloom so strong that the luminous feature loses shape.
- Do not use bright scratches, sparks, dust, text, and lights as five competing focal systems.
- Do not infer actual material, construction method, lens, or render engine from a visual resemblance.
